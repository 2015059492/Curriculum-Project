Station 4: Apply Zone

Welcome to the Apply Zone! In this station, you will apply your understanding of immunity to real-world public health situations involving vaccination, disease outbreaks, and community protection.

Learning Goals

  • Explain how vaccines work
  • Describe immune memory
  • Explain herd immunity
  • Analyze the effects of low vaccination rates
  • Evaluate misinformation about vaccines
  • Design a public health awareness campaign

Materials Needed

Physical Materials

  • Poster paper
  • Markers or colored pencils
  • Sticky notes
  • Worksheets
  • Scissors and glue

Digital Materials

  • Canva
  • PowerPoint
  • Google Slides
  • WHO and CDC resources
  • Vaccination infographics and videos

Part 1: Vaccination Case Study

The Community Vaccination Crisis

A local community has recently experienced a decrease in childhood vaccination rates after misinformation about vaccines spread on social media.

Several months later, a measles outbreak occurs within the community. Clinics become overcrowded, many children become sick, and healthcare workers discover that most infected children were not vaccinated.

Doctors and public health officials begin educating families about immunity, vaccination, and disease prevention.

Case Study Questions

  1. How do vaccines help protect the body from disease?
  2. Why were vaccinated children less severely affected?
  3. What is immune memory?
  4. What is herd immunity and why is it important?
  5. How can misinformation affect public health?
  6. Why is vaccination important for protecting communities?

Part 2: Herd Immunity Investigation

Study how herd immunity works by researching how vaccination helps reduce the spread of infectious diseases.

Your investigation should explain:

  • How diseases spread
  • How vaccines reduce transmission
  • Why vulnerable individuals depend on herd immunity
  • What happens when vaccination rates decrease

You may present your findings as:

  • A short paragraph
  • A flowchart
  • A labelled diagram
  • An infographic

Part 3: Public Health Awareness Campaign

Your Challenge

Create an awareness campaign encouraging people to understand the importance of vaccination.

You may create:

  • A poster
  • An infographic
  • A short video
  • A social media campaign
  • A digital presentation

Your Campaign Must Include

  • Information about vaccines
  • Benefits of vaccination
  • Dangers of misinformation
  • Explanation of herd immunity
  • Disease prevention strategies
  • Visuals and scientific facts

Reflection Questions

  1. Why are vaccines considered one of the most important public health developments?
  2. How can misinformation about vaccines affect communities?
  3. Why is herd immunity important for protecting vulnerable people?
  4. How did this activity change or strengthen your understanding of vaccination?

Extension Activity (Optional)

Research one vaccine-preventable disease such as measles, polio, HPV, tuberculosis, or COVID-19.

Explain:

  • How the disease affects the body
  • How vaccines help prevent the disease
  • Why vaccination programs are important globally
