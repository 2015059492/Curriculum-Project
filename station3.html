Station 3: Build Zone

In this station, you will create a model showing how the immune system responds when harmful pathogens enter the body. This activity will help you visualize immune responses and understand how different parts of the immune system work together to protect the body from disease.

Learning Goals

  • Identify important components of the immune system
  • Explain the role of antibodies and white blood cells
  • Describe how pathogens are destroyed
  • Understand immune memory and long-term protection

Materials Needed

You may use physical or digital materials to build your model.

  • Paper or cardboard
  • Markers or colored pencils
  • Glue or tape
  • Clay, beads, or string
  • Canva, Google Slides, or PowerPoint (optional)

Your Task

Create a model that demonstrates how the immune system responds to infection. Your model must include:

  1. A pathogen entering the body
  2. The immune system recognizing the pathogen
  3. White blood cells responding
  4. Antibodies attaching to pathogens
  5. The destruction of pathogens
  6. Memory cells remaining after infection

Build Instructions

  1. Choose whether you want to create a physical or digital model.
  2. Sketch a rough design before building.
  3. Label all important immune system components clearly.
  4. Use arrows or symbols to show the sequence of immune responses.
  5. Use different colors to represent pathogens, antibodies, and immune cells.
  6. Prepare a short explanation of your model for presentation.

Guiding Questions

  1. How does the immune system recognize harmful pathogens?
  2. Why are antibodies important?
  3. What is the role of memory cells?
  4. How do vaccines help the immune system?
  5. What could happen if the immune system fails to respond?

Reflection Activity

After completing your model, write a short reflection answering the following:

  1. Which part of the immune response was easiest to model?
  2. Which part was most difficult to explain visually?
  3. How did building the model improve your understanding of immunity?

Extension Activity

Research one immune-related disease such as HIV/AIDS, allergies, lupus, or COVID-19. Explain how the immune system is affected and describe possible treatments or prevention strategies.
